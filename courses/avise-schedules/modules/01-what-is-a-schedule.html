Many names, one pattern

In accounting this pattern has many names: amortization (assets), recognition (revenue), accruals (expenses). The schedule is the tool that does the math and records the entries automatically.

The Two Accounts Involved

Every schedule entry is a journal entry — a debit and a credit. At least one of those accounts must be a Balance Sheet account — that is the source being drained.

The rule that makes this accounting

At least one account must be a Balance Sheet account. Without one, there is nothing to drain — the schedule would just move money between two expense buckets with no tracking of what remains.

Three Ways to Calculate Each Entry

The calculation method controls how the total amount is divided into individual period amounts. Choose based on how precise the pro-rating needs to be.

📅
Day

Calculates a daily rate, then multiplies by the exact number of days in each period. Partial months are handled precisely — no rounding shortcuts.

Example
$12,000 ÷ 365 = $32.88/day
Jan (31 days) → $1,019.18
Feb (28 days) → $919.73
Best for partial months
🗓️
Month

Divides the total into equal monthly amounts. If the schedule starts or ends mid-month, those boundary periods are pro-rated by day count. The default method.

Example
$12,000 ÷ 12 = $1,000/month
Jan (full) → $1,000
Feb (full) → $1,000
Default — most common
⚙️
Custom

You define the number of entries and their cadence — monthly, quarterly, or annually. Useful when the payment schedule does not follow standard calendar divisions.

Example
4 quarterly entries
Q1 → $3,000 · Q2 → $3,000
Q3 → $3,000 · Q4 → $3,000
Non-standard schedules

Simple vs Multi-Line Schedules

Not all journal entries are created equal. Sometimes a single debit and credit is enough — other times a payment needs to be split across many accounts simultaneously.

📄
Simple Schedule

One debit account and one credit account. Every entry looks identical except for its date and possibly its amount. Perfect for straightforward prepaid expenses and deferred revenues.

Multi-Line Schedule

Each entry can have multiple debits and multiple credits. Needed whenever a single expense must be allocated across departments or when a payment covers several services at once.
